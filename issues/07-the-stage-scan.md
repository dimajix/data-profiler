# 07 — The stage scan

**Status:** in progress
**Type:** feature
**Priority:** high
**Area:** Scan
**Epic:** [02](02-first-version-epic.md)
**Summary:** Nothing counts what the columns of a table hold - their nulls, their distinct values, their ranges, whether they are enums - and nothing takes a sample of them.

## The problem

The stage `scan` is the first the tool runs (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, "How it works"): for each
selected table, the statistics of all columns in one query, the values of every column with few
distinct ones, and a sample into the workspace. None of it exists, nor the command line that runs
it.

## Why it matters

Every later stage works from what `scan` found, and it is the stage that loads the database: tables
of hundreds of millions of rows, which may be read whole, but not grouped by a column of UUIDs.

## How

In steps, each reviewed and committed on its own:

1.  **Statistics.** One query per table for all its columns: rows, nulls, `approx_distinct`, the
    range of numbers and dates, the range of lengths of texts - written to the profile
    ([03](03-the-profile.md)).
2.  **Enums.** The values and their counts of every column whose approximate distinct count is
    below the enum threshold, by `GROUP BY` - never on a column above it.
3.  **Samples.** Rows taken by `TABLESAMPLE BERNOULLI`, its share computed from the row count,
    kept in the workspace as Parquet; the workspace keeps itself out of version control by a
    `.gitignore` of its own.
4.  **The command line.** `data-profiler scan`, with the URL, the selection of tables, the
    profile and the workspace; `.env`; the log to standard error (`docs/CODING-GUIDELINES.md`,
    "Logging"). `README.md` says how the tool is run.

Things to settle on the way:

- **Values before detection.** The profile holds raw values only for columns of no sensitivity,
  but sensitivity is found by [08](08-detection.md). Until then, `scan` writes no raw values into
  the profile - no enum values, no ranges -, and keeps them in the workspace;
  [08](08-detection.md) moves into the profile what it finds to be not sensitive.
- **The threshold of an enum.** A constant at first (500 distinct values); a setting once someone
  needs another.
- **The round trip.** A profile generated by [05](05-the-simulation-environment.md), loaded, and
  scanned again gives the same columns, types and shares of nulls, within the error of
  `approx_distinct`.

## Done when

- `data-profiler scan` writes a profile of the selected tables of the simulation environment, and
  their samples into the workspace.
- No raw value of the database is in the profile.
- The round trip holds.

## History

- **2026-10-29** — Opened with the plan of the first version ([02](02-first-version-epic.md)).

- **2026-10-29** — Step 1 done, as agreed with the maintainer: `scan(source, pattern)` in
  `data_profiler.scan` makes the profile of the tables a pattern matches, each counted by one query
  the new `Dialect.statistics` writes - its rows, and for each column its nulls, its distinct values
  and the range of the lengths of its texts, in a row of fixed form, so that the stage needs no case
  for the types; the dialect tells which are texts. The ranges of numbers and dates are not counted
  before [08](08-detection.md) can let them into the profile. The round trip with the example of the
  simulation found `approx_distinct` 5.7 percent off in one column, at its default standard error of
  2.3 percent; the dialect asks for half a percent, which costs Trino about 64 KB per column and
  came out within a quarter of a percent. The fixtures of the simulation moved into
  `tests/conftest.py`, as two test modules use them now.

- **2026-10-29** — Step 1 changed after the review, as the maintainer decided: the dialect no longer
  writes the statement of the statistics whole, which left the form of its row described in `source`
  and read in `scan`, held together by a convention of four values per column. Weighed: a
  `Source.statistics` that counts and reads, as `tables` and `columns` do - rejected by the
  maintainer, as tables and columns are metadata, which belong to the source, while what the tables
  hold is something else. Instead the dialect offers building blocks - `select`, `rows`, `nulls`,
  `distinct`, `shortest`, `longest`, `is_text` -, and the new module `data_profiler.statistics`
  builds the query from them and reads its row along the same list of expressions, so that the form
  of the row has one owner; lengths are asked only of texts. It is a module of its own from the
  start, so that the pattern is set for the counting to come.

- **2026-10-29** — Step 1 done. The maintainer confirmed half a percent as the standard error of the
  distinct values, and a method `Source.name` for the name of a table with its catalog and schema,
  which the messages of the source and of `statistics` both need. After a second review,
  `statistics` decides the expressions of each column once and reads the row along them; a counted
  value that is no number is a broken invariant of the dialect, a `TypeError` naming the column.
